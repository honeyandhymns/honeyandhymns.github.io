5 Free Public Domain Books for a Charlotte Mason Homeschool

Discover five beautiful living books you can read for free in your Charlotte Mason homeschool using public domain classics.

✦

Over the past few weeks, I’ve been sharing my growing love for the Charlotte Mason method — and honestly, the more I learn, the more excited I get. One of my favorite discoveries has been the idea of living books: rich, well-written stories that bring a subject to life instead of just handing kids dry facts to memorize. There’s something magical about watching a child fall in love with a story instead of just checking a box on a curriculum.

Here’s the best part — so many of these classics are completely free! Because they’re in the public domain, you can find them on sites like Project Gutenberg and Many Books (Which I discovered through Ambleside Online). Just download them as an .epub file and read them on your Kindle or e-reader of choice. No cost, no excuses — just great books waiting to be opened.

So today I want to share five living books I’ve downloaded and can’t wait to add to our homeschool this year.

1. Alice in Wonderland

Okay, friend, if you do nothing else this year — add this one. There is something about Lewis Carroll’s absurd, dreamy world that just delights kids in a way that feels almost old-fashioned magic. It’s whimsical, it’s clever, and it stretches their imagination in ways a workbook simply can’t. Your kids will fall down the rabbit hole right alongside Alice, and I promise you’ll catch yourself smiling through half of it too. This is the kind of book that makes childhood feel like childhood.

2. Aesop’s Fables

You know how we’re always looking for those little character-building moments in our homeschool day? This is basically a treasure chest of them. Aesop’s Fables are short, simple, and packed with wisdom our kids can actually carry with them — kindness, honesty, patience, all wrapped up in stories about foxes and tortoises and crows. They’re perfect for a quick read-aloud during morning basket time, and I promise the lessons will stick far longer than any lecture ever could.

3. The Wizard of Oz

Friend, this one is just pure joy. L. Frank Baum’s story of Dorothy and her ragtag friends is an adventure your kids will beg to keep reading “just one more chapter” of. It’s got courage, friendship, and the beautiful reminder that we often already have what we’re looking for inside us. It’s the kind of story that plants itself in a child’s imagination and never really leaves — I still think of the Yellow Brick Road decades later, and I want that same kind of memory for my kids.

4. Beautiful Stories from Shakespeare

I know, I know — Shakespeare feels intimidating. But trust me on this one. This retelling takes the heart of his greatest plays and makes them approachable and captivating for young listeners, without losing the beauty of the language. It’s such a gentle, lovely way to introduce our kids to something they’ll encounter again and again throughout their education. Give it a try — you might just find your kids asking for “the one about the fairies” (Midsummer Night’s Dream, be still my heart) again and again.

5. The Burgess Bird Book for Children

If you’re wanting to weave a little nature study into your read-aloud time, this one is an absolute gem. Thornton Burgess has this gift for turning birds into real characters with real personalities, so kids fall in love with them the way they’d fall in love with any storybook friend. By the end, your children won’t just know facts about birds — they’ll actually care about them. It’s living science at its very best, and I think you’re going to love it as much as I do.

These five are just the beginning, but I truly think they’re the perfect place to start. Happy reading, friend!
